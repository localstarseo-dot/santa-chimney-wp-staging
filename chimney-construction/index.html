How Does a New Chimney Project Get Started?

A new chimney needs to fit the home and the fireplace or appliance it serves. Review the project requirements before a construction scope and schedule are agreed.

Step 1

Review the Project and Property

Our team starts by reviewing the proposed chimney project, the property layout, and the intended fireplace or appliance.

For a new home, renovation, or fireplace addition, homeowners can provide building plans, appliance details, and the proposed installation location.

This information helps establish the starting requirements for the project.

01
Step 2

Review the Chimney Route and System

The proposed chimney route must be compatible with the appliance and the building.

Important considerations include the chimney's location, required clearances, flue configuration, surrounding structure, and roofline.

The proposed arrangement must also allow for appropriate venting and weather protection.

02
Step 3

Define the Construction Scope

Once the project requirements are understood, the proposed construction work can be defined.

Depending on the approved system, the scope may involve masonry construction, the chimney flue or liner, chimney termination, a crown or cap, and the connection where the chimney passes through the roof.

Fireplace installation, structural modifications, roofing work, and other trades must be identified separately when applicable.

03
Step 4

Confirm Responsibilities and Approvals

Before construction proceeds, the project scope, responsibilities, required approvals, and applicable building requirements need to be established.

Santa Chimney Sweep reviews the proposed service with the homeowner so the agreed work and any separate responsibilities are clear.

Construction and completion requirements depend on the approved design, project conditions, and applicable local codes.

04

When Does Your Chimney Need More Than Sweeping?

Not every chimney problem can be solved by cleaning.

Heavy Creosote Buildup

Thick or hardened deposits may need specialized removal rather than routine sweeping.

Chimney Damage

Cracked liners, broken masonry, and other damaged components need evaluation and possible repair.

Explore Chimney Repair

Unknown Smoke Problems

If smoke enters your room, cleaning may help only when removable buildup is the cause.

Explore Chimney Inspections

Dirty Fireplace or Firebox

Fireplace cleaning focuses on ash and residue in the fireplace area. It is different from sweeping the chimney flue.

Know What Is Involved Before Building

A chimney project involves the appliance, venting route, chimney structure, and surrounding home. Understand the proposal before approving construction.

Review the Home and Chimney Requirements

A chimney for a new house may have different requirements from one being added to an existing home.

The proposed fireplace, chimney type, building layout, and roof configuration help determine which construction options may be suitable.

Clarify the Construction Responsibilities

A new chimney project may involve masonry, venting, roofing, appliance installation, structural work, and permits.

The construction scope should identify which services Santa Chimney Sweep will provide and which responsibilities belong to the homeowner or another qualified contractor.

Review Changes Before Proceeding

If the proposed construction scope changes after further evaluation, the additional requirements should be explained before work proceeds.

Homeowners can review the proposed changes and approve the next steps based on the actual project needs.

Questions Homeowners Ask About Chimney Construction

Clear answers about new chimney projects, masonry and brick construction, project costs, rebuilding, fireplaces, and service availability.

Do I need new construction or rebuilding?

New construction is generally for homes that need a chimney where one does not already exist.

If your home has a damaged chimney, repair or rebuilding may be more appropriate. The existing structure should be evaluated before deciding whether reconstruction or a new installation is needed.

Explore Chimney Rebuilding

Can a chimney be added to an existing home?

It may be possible, depending on the home's structure, fireplace or appliance type, available space, and proposed chimney route.

The property and system requirements must be reviewed before a construction approach can be confirmed.

Is brick or masonry construction available?

Brick and masonry construction may be suitable for certain projects.

The available options depend on the intended appliance, chimney design, structural requirements, and construction services supported for the property.

Will the project need permits or inspections?

New chimney construction may require building permits and inspections, depending on the property and project scope.

The City of Austin provides residential permit requirements and application guidance. Confirm the required approvals and who will handle them before construction begins.
