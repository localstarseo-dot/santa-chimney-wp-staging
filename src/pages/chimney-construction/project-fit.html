When New Construction Makes Sense

Does Your Project Actually Need a New Chimney?

Not every chimney project is new construction. If there is already a chimney on the home, repair, rebuilding, or restoration may make more sense.

New construction is mainly for projects that need a chimney that is not already there.

You’re Adding a New Fireplace

A new fireplace may need a chimney or another venting setup, depending on the fireplace and the home. The fireplace and chimney should be planned together rather than treated as unrelated projects.

See Fireplace Installation

You’re Building a New Home

A chimney may be part of the fireplace or heating plans for a new home. The setup can depend on the fireplace or appliance, home layout, roofline, and overall project design.

You’re Making a Major Addition or Renovation

A large remodel can change where a fireplace sits, how the roof is laid out, or where a chimney can run. If the current chimney cannot work with the new layout, a new chimney may need to be considered.

The Home Does Not Have a Chimney

If you want to add a fireplace or supported appliance to a home without an existing chimney, the property needs to be looked at first. Where the chimney could go, what it will serve, and how it reaches the roof all matter.

You Want a New Masonry Chimney

Some homeowners are specifically planning a brick or masonry chimney. Tell us what you have in mind so the materials, construction type, and fit for the project can be confirmed.

The Existing Chimney Is Too Damaged

A badly damaged chimney does not automatically become a new construction project. If the chimney already exists, repair, rebuilding, or restoration may be the more appropriate path.

See Chimney Rebuilding
