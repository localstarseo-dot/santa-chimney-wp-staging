What Happens During Wood Stove Service?

Santa Chimney Sweep reviews the stove, its connecting pipe, and related chimney concerns to determine the appropriate service. Cleaning, inspection, repair, and installation involve different work depending on the stove model and condition.

Step 1

Review the Stove and Reported Concern

Our team starts by reviewing the wood stove type, make or model, and the problem you have noticed.

Heavy ash, smoke entering the room, damaged components, and installation requests require different evaluations.

This information helps determine which parts of the system need attention.

01
Step 2

Review Accessible Connected Components

Technicians review the accessible areas connected to the service request.

Depending on the concern, these may include the firebox, visible internal components, door seals, glass, connecting pipe, and chimney connection.

Visible deterioration, accumulated deposits, and damaged venting components help determine whether cleaning, inspection, or repair is appropriate.

02
Step 3

Complete the Approved Service

The work depends on the supported stove system and the approved service scope.

Cleaning may involve removing ash, soot, or loose deposits from accessible stove areas included in the appointment.

Repair work may involve addressing damaged or worn components when suitable replacement parts and repair methods are available.

Connecting-pipe replacement, chimney sweeping, and stove installation are separate work scopes that must match the system's requirements.

03
Step 4

Review Results and Remaining Concerns

After the service, technicians review the areas or components addressed.

The team explains the completed work and identifies any remaining concerns involving the stove, connecting pipe, or chimney.

If additional cleaning, replacement, or further inspection is recommended, the next service is discussed separately.

04

Questions Homeowners Ask About Wood Stove Services

Is wood stove cleaning the same as chimney sweeping?

Wood stove cleaning addresses deposits and ash in the stove areas covered by the service.

Chimney sweeping removes soot, creosote, and loose debris from the chimney's accessible venting path. Both may be needed, but they should not be assumed to be included in one appointment.

Explore Chimney Sweeping

Do you review wood stove repairs?

Santa Chimney Sweep reviews wood stove repair requests for supported systems.

Repair options depend on the stove brand, model, damaged component, and availability of compatible replacement parts.

Share the model information and a description of the problem so the team can confirm the available service.

Should the chimney be inspected annually?

Yes. The Chimney Safety Institute of America recommends inspecting chimneys and venting systems at least once a year and performing cleaning or repairs when necessary.

A wood stove's inspection and maintenance needs also depend on its use and manufacturer instructions.

Read CSIA Homeowner Guidance

When should I stop using the stove?

Stop using the stove if smoke enters the room, the flue or connecting pipe appears damaged, or you suspect a previous chimney fire.

Have the system evaluated before using it again. If a chimney fire is actively occurring, evacuate and call emergency services.

Read CSIA Chimney Fire Prevention Guidance
