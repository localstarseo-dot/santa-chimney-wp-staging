What Happens During Chimney Repair?

Santa Chimney Sweep evaluates damaged chimney components to determine the appropriate repair. The work depends on the location of the problem, the materials involved, and how much of the chimney has been affected.

Step 1

Review the Reported Damage

Our technicians review the reported problem and examine the accessible chimney areas related to your concern.

They look for visible cracks, deteriorated mortar, water entry, loose components, and signs of damage that may affect the repair.

01
Step 2

Evaluate the Affected Components

The team evaluates the affected components to help determine what is causing the damage.

For chimney leaks, this may involve checking the crown, flashing, masonry, chimney cap, or chase cover.

For structural or flue concerns, the team reviews the damaged areas and determines whether additional inspection is necessary.

02
Step 3

Explain and Approve the Repair

The recommended work depends on the condition of the affected components.

Repairs may involve replacing damaged bricks, restoring mortar joints, repairing or replacing a chimney crown, correcting flashing problems, replacing a damaged chase cover, or addressing flue and liner damage.

The team explains the recommended work before the homeowner approves the repair.

03
Step 4

Complete and Review the Work

Technicians complete the approved work according to the identified repair scope.

Afterward, the team reviews the completed repair and explains any additional concerns that may need attention.

If damage extends beyond the original repair area, further work is discussed separately.

04

Questions Homeowners Ask About Chimney Repair

Do I need chimney repair or an inspection?

If you notice cracked masonry, water entry, damaged components, or a repair problem already identified by a professional, chimney repair is a suitable starting service.

If the cause is unclear, an inspection can help identify the problem before the appropriate repair is recommended.

Explore Chimney Inspections

Can you help if I do not know where the leak starts?

Yes. You do not need to identify the leaking component before contacting Santa Chimney Sweep.

Water may enter through flashing, crowns, masonry, caps, chase covers, or nearby roof areas. The affected areas need to be evaluated before the appropriate repair can be determined.

Explore Chimney Leak Repair

Does a damaged chimney always need rebuilding?

Not every damaged chimney needs to be rebuilt.

Localized damage may be repairable, while widespread masonry deterioration, structural movement, or extensive damage may require rebuilding or restoration.

The condition of the affected components determines the recommended approach.

Can I use the fireplace if I suspect damage?

If you notice falling flue tiles, loose masonry inside the fireplace, smoke entering the room, or suspected chimney-fire damage, stop using the fireplace until a qualified professional evaluates it.

Damaged chimney components can affect the system's ability to vent combustion gases safely.

Read CSIA Chimney Fire Safety Guidance

What should I provide when booking a repair?

Tell us what damage you've noticed, where it appears, and your property ZIP code.

Mention any recent storms, water leaks, falling debris, smoke problems, or findings from a previous chimney inspection. Photos of visible damage can also help explain your concern.
